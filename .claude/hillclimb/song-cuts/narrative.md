Current cutter: v10, held-out 72.2 against 34.5 at baseline (+37.7). Deltas for rounds 5-7 are versus v3. Bar alignment (outer step 2) is the largest single gain: one CLAP embedding per bar, so every candidate cut sits on a bar start. Three bar sources score the same within noise on the 3-second target (human 67.7, tracker trained on these songs 68.2, tracker that never saw the song 67.9), so the gain is not an artifact of annotation-side information; the fair tracker costs about 2.5 points on the half-second score. Controls: the same number of cuts on random bar starts scores 23.3, evenly spaced cuts snapped to bars 24.4. Before bars, the best was v3 at 50.8 (+16.3). Both kept changes are about where a cut is reported, not which cuts are made: v1 moved cuts to the middle of the window overlap, and v3 lets a cut fall between the 5 s grid points. v2 made more cuts, which helped the songs it was tuned on and not the held-out ones.

The analyzer's reading of the train logs is that timing on a coarse grid is still the main loss: re-scoring v1's cuts at a 5 s tolerance gave 66 against 47 at 3 s. With v3 in place, the remaining quick settings gain at most 1.4 on train (threshold, offset 3.0), so the inner loop is saturated on these embeddings. The next lever is the grid itself: finer window spacing, which needs re-embedding.

Round 4 (outer step 1) re-embedded every song with a window every 2 s. It did not help: once round 3 lets a cut fall between grid points, a finer grid adds nothing, and with 80% overlap between neighbouring windows the change score gets blurrier. A follow-up idea, keeping the two compared sides from sharing audio (a "guard"), was swept on train only: at 5 s spacing a 5 s guard gives 53.1 against 52.8; at 2 s spacing the best guard (4 s) gives 53.4. Both are inside noise on the target, so no round was spent, though the half-second score rose from 8.9 to about 11-12 in those sweeps. The remaining timing error therefore looks like a property of the 10 s window itself, not of the grid.

Comparison row (separate flow ../song-cuts-mel/, all 912 songs): the same round-3 cutter fed plain spectrogram features scores 41.8 on the 154 shared held-out songs against 50.8 for CLAP (-9.0 ±3.2).

Tried on train only after v7, no round spent: adding a per-bar note-content (chroma) change score to CLAP's. Chroma alone scores 48.9 on train (CLAP 68.8); combined at weights 1:0.1 / 1:0.25 / 1:0.5 / 1:1 it scores 69.3 / 68.1 / 66.2 / 62.8. The best is +0.5, inside noise, and more chroma hurts. A pitch-shift probe (scripts/probe_pitch.py) had suggested CLAP is only weakly sensitive to key, but a chroma change score does not add usable boundary information here; chords change within sections, so harmonic change is not section change. Harmony may still matter through repetition rather than change.

Inner loop on the bar version (train only, no rounds spent): the analyzer re-scored v7's train cuts (928 hits, 341 false cuts, 501 misses; precision 0.73, recall 0.65) and simulated peak-rule changes from the logs: shorter minimum gap, a fixed number of cuts, local-contrast thresholds, snapping to 2/4/8-bar grids, moving plateau cuts later. All landed within about 1 point. Its only candidate, dropping cuts in the last 4 bars, gains 1.1 and about half of that is a scoring artefact. Change-score variants run on the embeddings: no centring 62.1; Gaussian taper 68.7; scales 8+16 s 68.9; 8+16+32 s 68.0 (current 68.8). The inner loop is saturated on these embeddings. The analyzer attributes the remaining loss to the change score itself: 188 missed boundaries have a peak below threshold and 172 have no peak at all, and peak height does not separate true from false peaks.

Rounds 8 and 9: block matching alone ties the change score on the target while making 13.6 cuts per song against 8.2, and is better at the half-second tolerance (+4.1 ±3.0 on test). Letting block matching propose cuts and the change score veto them (keep a cut only where the change score is above the song's median positive value) was the best of six combinations simulated on train. On held-out it gives +2.1 ±2.2 on the target, just inside noise, and +6.1 ±2.6 on the half-second score (train +6.0 ±2.4). Under the keep rule, which is stated on the target, it is not proven and was first reverted. On 2026-11-12 the user chose to keep it for the half-second gain, knowing this overrides the rule after seeing the result; the target gain stays reported as within noise. The half-second gain is real on both halves, so it is the better cutter if fine timing is the goal. Untrimmed on all 307 songs v9 scores 76.1 / 58.5 (3 s / 0.5 s) against 74.4 / 54.0 for v7.

Round 12 (outer step 6) was built by a subagent that saw train songs only: boosted trees over about 100 features of each block-matching proposal (segment lengths, similarity of the segments either side, repeats 4/8/16 bars ahead, recurrence, position, change scores, the same from CLAP, and PCA levels), replacing the median veto (scripts/merge_model.py, data/merge_model.npz, setting cbm_merge_model). Its own 5-fold out-of-fold estimate on train was +0.7 ±1.3 with nested selection over 12 configurations and +2.1 ±1.3 for the configuration finally used (selection-biased); shuffled labels gave -4.1. On the 154 held-out songs, which the model never saw, it scores 75.4 against 72.2: +3.2 ±1.1, half-second 55.3 against 52.5, same number of cuts. The held-out result is the clean one and clears noise; the train-side evidence is weaker than the rule asks for. It also changes what the cutter is: it is now fitted to Harmonix annotators' section conventions on 153 songs, so it is no longer training-free and may not transfer to other annotation styles or genres. cuts.json is left at v10 until the user decides.

Beat-level step (outer step 7; train only, no round spent because no candidate moved the target). Beats come from the same fold-held-out tracker (scripts/hx_beats.py); one bar of audio starting at every beat was embedded with MATPAC++ (scripts/hx_embed_matpac_beats.py). Diagnosis on train: 98% of annotated boundaries lie within 0.15 s of a tracked beat but only 78% on a tracked bar start; 165 lie one beat before a bar start and 111 two beats after; 15 of 153 songs have most boundaries off the tracked phase. Upper bounds using the annotations: the best bar phase per song gives 74.9 / 59.9 (3 s / half-second) against 73.9 / 54.0; moving each kept cut to the best beat within two beats gives 77.0 / 67.0. Audio-only versions do not reach either. Choosing the phase by block-matching score, score per bar, change strength at the cuts or change-score peaks moves 72-96% of songs and drops the half-second score to 17-31. Moving a cut to a neighbouring beat when the local change between the bar before and the bar after is stronger there gives at best 73.9 / 55.4 (+1.4 at half a second, inside noise) across 36 settings. One-bar embeddings one beat apart are too alike to say which beat a section starts on. Counted as a failed outer step. The ceilings say beat placement is worth up to 13 points at half a second, so it remains the largest known loss; it likely needs a finer signal (per-beat features or vocal onsets) or a learned placer.

Beat placement, second pass (train only, no rounds spent). Training-free with finer signals (scripts/hx_beat_feats.py): per-beat spectrum change, onset strength and loudness rise all lower the half-second score unless restricted to moving almost nothing (best 54.0 against 54.0; onset 49.4, loudness 46.8); MATPAC++ embeddings of single beats give at best 73.7 / 55.1 (+1.1 at half a second, inside noise). Learned placer built by a subagent on train songs (scripts/beat_placer.py, data/beat_placer.npz; a 23-weight conditional logit over the five candidate beats plus a song-level pass): nested out-of-fold +0.0 to +0.2 at 3 s and +1.4 to +1.7 ±1.6-1.7 at half a second over three seeds, with a shuffled-target control reproducing about half of that. It finds some half-bar errors (46 of 107) and almost no pickups (11 of 103), and wrongly moves 40 of 774 correct cuts. Not scored on held-out: nothing cleared noise on train and the 3-second target did not move. Two measurement notes from that work: the median beat is 0.50 s, so a one-beat error sits right at the half-second tolerance; and five train songs have annotations a median 70-167 ms off the tracked beat, which looks like residual alignment error. Both beat-placement attempts count as failed outer steps: two in a row, so by the stopping rule the climb is saturated.
